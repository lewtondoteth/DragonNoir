# Chapter 1 Office Entry and Coffee Ritual Draft

Rough working draft - needs refinement before integration.

This file preserves the office-entry and coffee-ritual material from Issue #2 as an experimental scene draft. It is not the canonical Chapter 1 text yet.

## Purpose

- Preserve the toy-world coffee ritual discovery.
- Test slower office-entry pacing after the pink dragon doorway reveal.
- Keep the ritual separate from the canonical chapter until the transition and similarity reveal are refined.

## Toy-World Rule Preserved Here

Toy rituals are emotionally real even when physically performative.

The coffee sounds should come from Dragon carrying out the ritual sincerely. The pink dragon should understand and normalize the ritual, not treat it as strange. The scene should never wink at the audience or describe the ritual as pretend.

## Draft Text

"This," I said, "is already the strangest thing the weather's done to me all week."

The pink dragon stood quietly in the doorway while the rain gathered along the brim of her little hat and fell in soft ticks onto the hallway floor.

Behind me, the office waited in all its professional disappointment.

Behind her, Toy City dissolved slowly into rain.

Neither option looked especially responsible.

"You'd better come in," I said eventually.

This was less hospitality than structural concern. Enough rain gets into a hallway and the whole building starts developing opinions.

She stepped inside carefully.

Small steps.

Same as mine.

The door shut behind her with a tired little click and the room immediately felt smaller in a way the architecture had not approved beforehand.

The rain continued at the windows.

Persistent.

Patient.

The sort of weather that expected eventually to win on principle.

She stood in the middle of the office for a moment taking the place in quietly.

The desk.

The paperwork.

The blanket still collapsed on the floor where it had lost the argument with morning.

The trifle bowl catching light beside the lamp like physical evidence.

None of this improved my position.

I rescued a chair from beneath a leaning stack of paperwork and nudged it toward her with my foot.

"Sit down."

She did.

Neatly.

Some toys fall into chairs. Others arrive there through careful negotiations with gravity and posture. She folded into the seat like she had rehearsed being sensible professionally.

Again, unsettling.

I looked at her for another second.

Same paws.

Same floppy arms.

Same little horns.

Same kind of dragon.

The morning continued refusing to explain itself.

"Coffee?" I asked.

"Please."

I moved toward the little kitchenette in the corner of the office.

The machine beside the sink was red plastic with a crooked steam wand and a silver sticker pretending to be a pressure gauge. Most of the buttons had been pressed smooth over the years. One of them leaned sideways slightly, exhausted by responsibility.

Behind me, she sat quietly with both paws folded in her lap while the rain moved softly against the windows.

The office waited.

So did the morning.

I removed the little portafilter from the machine.

Click.

A brief inspection followed.

Some routines survive because the alternative would be admitting conditions.

I tamped the grounds flat with the back of a spoon, locked the handle back into place, slid a cup underneath, and pressed the button.

"Chk-tchk-tchk-tchk," I said quietly.

The sound settled into the room beside the rain.

Better already.

I adjusted the cup slightly while the coffee continued.

Across the office, she watched without smiling.

Not confused.

Not amused.

Just watching the coffee being made.

That helped more than expected.

I picked up the little milk jug, held it beneath the steam wand, turned the dial, and said:

"Ffffffssshhhhh."

Steam filled the office in the proper professional quantities.

Her eyes followed the jug for a moment.

"Good foam," she said.

"The machine still has its moments."

"Clearly."

I poured the milk carefully into the first cup.

"Glug-glug-glug."

Then the second.

"Glug-glug-glug."

Tiny circles near the top.

There was no point abandoning standards completely.

I carried one cup across the office and handed it to her.

Our paws brushed briefly during the exchange.

Same shape.

Same soft plush at the wrists.

Same slight inward pull in the stitching.

Familiar enough to feel strange.

She accepted the cup with a small nod and lifted it carefully toward her mouth.

"Glug-glug."

Careful little sips.

Good manners.

I sat down behind the desk again and drank from my own cup.

"Glug."

A pause.

"Glug."

The headache behind my button eye lowered its voice slightly.

Across the desk, she lowered the cup again, reached up slowly, and removed the little rain hat first.

Then the coat.

Folded carefully.

Neatly arranged over the back of the chair.

The similarities became much harder to ignore after that.
