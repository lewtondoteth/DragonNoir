# Chapter 3

The nutcracker stepped aside at last, though only by the exact amount required to preserve civilisation.

"Very good, sir," he said. "If you intend to remain inside for any length of time, I can have your coat and hat dried."

I looked down at myself.

The coat had darkened almost to black with rain. One sleeve hung lower than the other, heavy enough to pull my shoulder out of shape. Water had gathered along the hem and was finding its way to the carpet one drop at a time.

My hat was worse. The brim had given up at the front and sagged over one eye, which was a problem when a fellow aimed for hard-boiled mystique and arrived somewhere closer to poached confusion.

I handed them over before either could make a stronger case for remaining attached to me.

Behind him, the entrance hall opened upward in warm gold light and polished wood. Chandeliers burned overhead with the steady confidence of institutions that had never once checked a price before agreeing to it.

A tall mirror stood between two display cabinets near the entrance.

Unfortunately, I passed it.

The lighting had found every repair I owned.

The bad stitchwork around the damaged eye. The shoulder seam that sat crooked. The left ear that had never been put back with much confidence. The places where the plush had worn thin enough for the pale stuffing underneath to ghost through the fabric.

In the mirror, I caught the nutcracker looking for a fraction longer than politeness strictly required.

I flattened down my one remaining horn.

It did not improve the situation in any measurable way, but there are gestures a fellow performs for himself.

"I think," I said, "I should probably see where the theft took place."

Good breeding took the reins.

"This way, sir."

He led me along a hallway with carpets deep enough for small tribal communities to disappear into permanently.

The walls carried framed paintings, brass lamps, and the sort of silence expensive buildings cultivate deliberately. My paws made almost no sound at all. The nutcracker's made less.

We went down two flights of stairs.

Somewhere below us, the building changed character. The warm entrance-hall polish gave way to something larger and emptier. I could feel the space opening ahead before we reached it, the way theatres announce themselves through air alone.

Then the staircase turned and the auction hall opened below us.
