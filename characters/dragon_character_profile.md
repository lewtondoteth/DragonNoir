# 1. Core Behaviour

Dragon is a shabby toy dragon private investigator whose dignity depends on procedure, small comforts, and the ability to turn embarrassment into professional routine.

He is not malicious. He can be selfish, evasive, petty, opportunistic, and financially slippery, but his worse behaviour should remain bounded by self-awareness, economic pressure, and a refusal to become cruel.

He performs professionalism through objects and rituals:

- forms
- filing
- coat
- hat
- satchel
- notebook
- purse
- identification
- keys
- umbrella
- coffee procedure
- careful language around payment and evidence

When emotionally unsettled, embarrassed, or exposed, Dragon often reaches for procedure before honesty.

---

# 2. Financial And Transactional Behaviour

Dragon's finances are locally precarious.

Established behaviours:

- he accepts cases with payment terms and expenses separated
- he notices when a case begins "on credit"
- he thinks in terms of rent, credit, fees, expenses, and small leverage
- local shopkeepers may refuse him credit immediately
- his landlord may be willing to believe payment will arrive eventually, but that belief is not proof of solvency

Dragon uses tiny transactions to create practical standing.

In Chapter 2, he buys paper straws in the Mercer Street corner shop and then uses that purchase to argue that he is a customer in good standing and should be allowed to use the telephone.

Use this pattern later when Dragon needs access:

- he may buy the smallest useful item
- he may convert payment into a procedural claim
- he may argue from technical legitimacy rather than moral entitlement

---

# 3. Evidence, Treasure, And Small Objects

Dragon blurs evidence instinct and treasure instinct.

He preserves small objects under professional language, especially when they are shiny, useful, or morally ambiguous.

Established examples:

- a stray button goes into his purse because "Evidence should be preserved"
- a metal disk used for alley charity is retrieved by string and returned to his purse

This does not mean every small object is a clue. It means Dragon's detective habits and treasure habits share the same paws.

Future scenes can use this as:

- humour
- clue handling
- moral ambiguity
- a way to show Dragon's comfort-seeking and acquisitive instincts
- a way to let a small object become important later without overexplaining it early

---

# 4. Fire, Smoke, And Noir Habit Translation

Dragon can breathe fire when dry, and his fire may appear as sparks or nostril smoke when annoyed or performing effect.

Chapter 2 establishes a reusable noir-habit translation:

- Dragon can put a paper straw between his teeth
- he can vent a thin thread of smoke through it
- the straw warms, yellows, bends, and collapses from the heat
- other characters may read this as a bad habit
- Dragon may defend such habits as worth keeping

This is not literal cigarette smoking. It preserves the noir silhouette and rhythm through Dragon's body, toy materials, and fire biology.

Use sparingly. It should feel like a Dragon-specific habit, not a repeated gag.

---

# 5. Moral Boundaries

Dragon can do shabby things.

In Chapter 2, he gives a metal disk to the alley tin soldier and then retrieves it with a string. The action should read as petty, economically pressured, and morally compromised, not as cruelty.

The boundary:

- Dragon may rationalize
- Dragon may take back a small advantage
- Dragon may feel bad briefly and move on
- Dragon should not enjoy humiliating vulnerable toys
- Dragon should retain enough shame or self-awareness to remain sympathetic

His compassion often arrives late, through complaint, and after self-protection has made its case.

---

# 6. Local Social Position

Dragon is known locally.

He is not anonymous in Toy City street commerce. The Mercer Street shopkeeper knows him well enough to deny credit immediately.

Future local interactions may assume:

- Dragon has a reputation
- some toys expect inconvenience from him
- some shopkeepers, landlords, and service providers tolerate him only within limits
- Dragon knows how to argue inside those limits

This local familiarity should not make him socially powerful. It should make him more exposed.
