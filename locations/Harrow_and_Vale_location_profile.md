# Harrow & Vale Location Profile

Canonical bounded location profile for Harrow & Vale in the Toy Noir Dragon continuity.

Use this profile alongside:

- `../chapters/chapter_01.md`
- `../chapters/chapter_02.md`
- `../chapters/chapter_03.md`
- `../locations/Toy_City_location_profile.md`
- `../rules/noir_narration_rules.md`

This profile preserves only the stable location and institution logic currently established in active Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 prose. It should not overdefine the interior, staff hierarchy, or deeper institutional politics before later prose earns them.

---

# 1. Core Function

Harrow & Vale is an expensive Toy City auction house.

In the current draft, it functions as:

- the institution tied to the missing music box case
- a wealthy, discreet sale house rather than a loud public marketplace
- a place where loss is converted into catalogue language and lot numbers
- Dragon's first strong contact point with collector-world embarrassment and institutional polish

It is not presented as a generic shop or gallery. It is a formal auction house with status, procedure, and expensive self-control.

---

# 2. Social And Institutional Feel

Harrow & Vale should feel:

- wealthy
- quiet
- controlled
- administratively confident
- collector-facing rather than street-facing
- embarrassed by disruption without ever wanting to admit embarrassment directly

Chapter 1 establishes it as one of the expensive houses:

- not the loud kind
- the quiet kind
- a place with carpet thick enough to muffle regret
- a place where estates are turned into lot numbers with polished indifference

Use that tone when the institution appears. It should imply preservation, money, taste, and social insulation.

---

# 3. Exterior Logic

Chapter 2 currently establishes the exterior as:

- pale stone
- tall windows
- expensive lighting
- a covered entrance with an awning
- an old, polished, heavy front door
- a narrow service alley running alongside the building
- enough frontage and pavement presence for police cars, barriers, and a cordoned scene
- upper architectural ornament, including stone gargoyles

The building should look expensive without becoming fantastical. Its authority comes from finish, scale, and self-assurance.

---

# 4. Interior Threshold And Route Logic

Chapter 3 currently establishes limited interior movement from the threshold toward the auction hall:

- the entrance hall opens upward in warm gold light and polished wood
- chandeliers provide bright institutional light
- a tall mirror stands between two display cabinets near the entrance
- the route to the auction hall passes along a quiet hallway with deep carpet, framed paintings, and brass lamps
- the auction hall is reached by going down two flights of stairs
- the lower part of the building feels larger, emptier, and more theatrical before the auction hall comes into view

Use these details as route and class-pressure logic, not as permission to over-map the building. The interior should expose Dragon's wear, dampness, and repair history through bright polish and controlled silence.

---

# 5. Staff Surface

Only limited staff logic is currently fixed:

- the front door may be answered by a formal servant figure rather than an openly welcoming clerk
- that servant can be controlled, cautious, subtly hostile, and protective of procedure
- the institution prefers to classify access rather than offer it

Do not invent a full staff roster, named officials, or interior departments unless later prose or issue scope requires them.

---

# 6. Case-Pressure Use

Harrow & Vale is useful for scenes involving:

- institutional embarrassment
- class discomfort
- private wealth presented as public procedure
- Dragon entering spaces that assume he does not belong there
- the contrast between polished surfaces and ugly practical events

The robbery pressure should feel socially expensive as much as criminal.

---

# 7. Use Limits

Do not overdefine Harrow & Vale yet.

Avoid adding:

- named internal rooms beyond what prose establishes
- a full floor plan
- named staff unless explicitly approved
- deeper institutional motives beyond the current case function
- decorative whimsy that weakens the house's controlled prestige

Until later prose says otherwise, preserve Harrow & Vale as a bounded exterior-and-threshold institution with strong class and procedural identity.
