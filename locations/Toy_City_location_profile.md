# 1. Core Setting

Toy City is a full living-toy metropolis.

The toys are real, the city is real, and the story does not need to apologise for either fact.

It is not merely:

- a hidden bedroom corner
- a secret society beneath human notice
- a metaphor that needs outside explanation

It is a proper city with:

- streets
- offices
- districts
- rumours
- institutions
- economies
- repair politics
- myths
- underworld networks

Human-made objects shape the city, but humans should not drive the main plot unless a specific future outline requires it.

---

# 2. Atmosphere

Toy City should feel:

- rain-slick
- lamp-lit
- handmade
- theatrical
- toy-scaled
- emotionally worn
- practically funny
- crowded with small lives
- built from discarded or repurposed objects

The city can be assembled from:

- toy boxes
- stage flats
- old shop displays
- model-town pieces
- dollhouse architecture
- cardboard signage
- tin roofs
- wooden blocks
- velvet curtains
- railway sets
- repurposed human objects

The rain matters.

For Dragon, every case in the rain is personal.

---

# 3. City Logic

Toys in Toy City live, work, trade, repair, argue, remember, forget, hide, and fear damage.

Setting rules:

- damaged toys can be repaired but may return changed
- replacement parts carry emotional weight
- some toys remember where they came from and some do not
- being forgotten is a social fear
- being replaced is an existential fear
- being disassembled is the closest thing to horror, and should be handled with restraint

Repair logic sits near the center of city life.

Repair shops can function as:

- hospitals
- tailors
- mechanics
- confessionals
- gossip exchanges
- black-market clinics
- places of care
- places of exploitation

Good repair preserves identity.

Bad repair erases, replaces, repurposes, or distorts it.

# 4. Districts

## The Lower Drawers

A cramped, damp district where old toys, loose parts, and forgotten things gather.

Known for:

- cheap repairs
- worse coffee
- better information

Dragon knows the Lower Drawers well and pretends not to.

## Button Market

A crowded trading district where buttons, beads, small gears, doll shoes, marbles, and questionable evidence change hands.

Treasure is everywhere. Dragon finds this professionally distracting.

## The Repair Quarter

A row of shops, clinics, back rooms, and official stitching houses.

Some are kind. Some are expensive. Some ask no questions because they already know the answers.

## Shelfside

A higher-status neighbourhood with cleaner fabric, better polish, fewer visible repairs, and more lies.

Odette comes from here, or claims to.

## The Lost-and-Found

A municipal office, warehouse, or mythic archive where missing toys are catalogued badly.

No one fully trusts it.

## The Feltworks

An industrial zone where stuffing, cloth, wire, thread, and replacement parts are processed.

It is useful for cases involving repair politics, labour, replacement parts, or hidden supply chains, but no specific conspiracy is fixed canon here yet.

## The Hearth District

A warmer, older area favored by fire-capable toys, mechanical toys, and toys that dislike damp.

Dragon prefers it but cannot afford to work there.

## The Rain Gutters

Dangerous channels and alleys where water gathers.

Dragon avoids them until a case makes that impossible.

# 5. Institutions

## Repair Shops

Repair shops are central to Toy City.

They should be treated as practical, emotional, and political spaces all at once.

## The Civic Toy Council

A respectable public body that claims to maintain order and protect toys.

Depending on the eventual case, it may be:

- honest
- compromised
- frightened
- useless
- quietly corrupt

## The Hero Office / Public Safety Bureau

A civic institution responsible for public rescues, ceremonies, and city morale.

Useful for stories about:

- public confidence
- manufactured bravery
- city ceremony
- the gap between official heroism and private courage

No staged-hero plot is fixed canon yet.

## The Stitchers' Guild

The official repair authority.

Some members are compassionate. Others may be vain, frightened, careless, corrupt, or simply trapped inside bad rules.

## Treasure Brokers

Dealers in old objects, shiny things, dragon hoards, and artifacts.

They should feel like part appraiser, part fence, part confessor, part opportunist.

# 6. Writing Guidance

Use Toy City as a living pressure system, not as painted scenery.

The city should affect:

- value
- damage
- dignity
- mobility
- weather exposure
- repair access
- professional risk
- who gets believed

When drafting city scenes:

- prefer practical details over abstract lore explanation
- let place reveal class, wear, fear, and social pressure through visible consequences
- keep toy-world logic sincere
- avoid flattening the city into generic noir backdrop

Toy City should feel complete enough that even small scenes imply larger civic life beyond the frame.
