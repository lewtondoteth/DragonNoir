# Dragon's Office Location Profile

Canonical location profile for Dragon's office in the Toy Noir Dragon continuity.

Use this profile alongside:

- `../chapters/chapter_01_opening.md`
- `../outlines/chapter_01_narrative_beats.md`
- `../rules/noir_narration_rules.md`

This document expands existing canon additively. It should preserve the office as a stable story location without forcing every future office scene to reuse identical clutter beats.

---

# 1. Core Function

Dragon's office is:

- his workplace
- his shelter from the rain
- a practical failure he still insists on calling professional
- a reflection of his standards and his inability to live up to them cleanly

It should feel like a detective office translated through toy-world pressure, dampness, and shabby dignity.

The office is not grand, sleek, or ironic.

It is a real working place that has been slowly defeated by:

- weather
- neglect
- cheap heat
- bad luck
- too much paper
- too little money
- Dragon continuing to use it anyway

---

# 2. Atmosphere

The office should usually feel:

- cramped
- damp
- overused
- mildly humiliating
- functional in stubborn fragments
- funny in practical ways
- sincere rather than whimsical

Important recurring pressures:

- water at the sill
- weak heat
- drafts
- inconvenient clutter
- paper that has absorbed too much weather
- furniture that objects to use

The room should undermine professionalism while still being the place where Dragon tries to perform it.

---

# 3. Physical Character

The office is not large.

It should read as arranged badly, though not randomly.

Expected traits:

- the desk is in the way in a way that suggests it has chosen to be
- papers lean, drift, wrinkle, or absorb damp
- loose objects may become hazards, irritants, or accidental evidence
- the radiator is weak, temperamental, and narratively useful
- the window is a constant source of weather pressure
- the room has just enough working order to stay open for business

The office should never feel cozy in a polished way.

It may occasionally feel sheltering, but the shelter is always imperfect.

---

# 4. Relationship To Dragon

The office reflects Dragon without becoming a blunt symbol.

Shared traits:

- visibly worn
- still functional
- embarrassing in detail
- stubbornly serviceable
- patched together through continued use rather than elegant restoration

The office should expose:

- Dragon's failed dignity
- his procedural habits
- his professional vanity
- his comfort-seeking
- his tolerance for practical decline so long as the place still mostly works

It is one of the main spaces where Dragon turns embarrassment into procedure.

---

# 5. Scene Use

The office is especially good for scenes involving:

- threshold shifts from weather into story
- procedural recovery after embarrassment or destabilization
- client interviews
- coffee-as-procedure
- professionalism being sabotaged by material reality
- quiet recognition scenes

When using the office, prefer:

- physical consequence
- object behaviour
- environmental interruption
- small indignities
- practical negotiation with space

Do not use the office as:

- a generic detective backdrop
- a purely comic mess
- a sentimental nest
- a stage set that stops affecting the scene once described

---

# 6. Writing Guidance

Do not require every office scene to repeat the same inventory of props.

Instead, preserve the stable logic:

- the office is compromised by weather
- it resists clean professionalism
- Dragon keeps working in it anyway
- the room participates in scenes through drafts, dampness, clutter, furniture, paper, and heat failure

Use different local details each time, but keep the same emotional and practical truth.
