# Dragon's Office Location Profile

Canonical location profile for Dragon's office in the Toy Noir Dragon continuity.

Use this profile alongside:

- `../chapters/chapter_01_opening.md`
- `../outlines/chapter_01_narrative_beats.md`
- `../rules/noir_narration_rules.md`

This document expands existing canon additively. It should preserve the office as a stable story location without forcing every future office scene to reuse identical clutter beats.

---

# 1. Core Function

Dragon's office is:

- his workplace
- his shelter from the rain
- a practical failure he still insists on calling professional
- a reflection of his standards and his inability to live up to them cleanly

It should feel like a detective office translated through toy-world pressure, dampness, and shabby dignity.

The office is not grand, sleek, or ironic.

It is a real working place that has been slowly defeated by:

- weather
- neglect
- cheap heat
- bad luck
- too much paper
- too little money
- Dragon continuing to use it anyway

---

# 2. Atmosphere

The office should usually feel:

- cramped
- damp
- overused
- mildly humiliating
- functional in stubborn fragments
- funny in practical ways
- sincere rather than whimsical

Important recurring pressures:

- water at the sill
- weak heat
- drafts
- inconvenient clutter
- paper that has absorbed too much weather
- furniture that objects to use

The room should undermine professionalism while still being the place where Dragon tries to perform it.

---

# 3. Physical Character

The office is not large.

It should read as arranged badly, though not randomly.

Expected traits:

- the desk is in the way in a way that suggests it has chosen to be
- papers lean, drift, wrinkle, or absorb damp
- loose objects may become hazards, irritants, or accidental evidence
- the radiator is weak, temperamental, and narratively useful
- the window is a constant source of weather pressure
- the room is roughly the size of a space meant for two desks and not much forgiveness
- one side of the room can feel more intact or professionally salvageable than the rest
- the room has just enough working order to stay open for business

The office should never feel cozy in a polished way.

It may occasionally feel sheltering, but the shelter is always imperfect.

---

# 4. Stable Physical Features

These features should usually remain true unless a draft explicitly changes them:

- there is one main desk that dominates the room and gets in the way
- there is a window that carries weather pressure into the scene
- there is a weak radiator near the window area
- there is guest seating that is usable but not comfortable
- there is a filing cabinet or similar storage mass near the radiator side
- there is at least one shelf or desk-edge area where Dragon's small treasures and working clutter can accumulate
- there is a kitchenette or coffee-making corner inside the office
- paper, files, and small desk objects are regularly present
- the room is narrow or cramped enough that movement through it matters

The exact clutter can vary. The room logic should not.

---

# 5. Geometry And Layout

Use a stable practical layout even when the prose does not list every feature.

Working assumptions:

- the door opens from the corridor directly into the office
- the desk sits far enough inside the room that entering toys must negotiate around it
- the guest chair or visitor position is on the far side of the desk
- the window and radiator create one main weather-pressure side of the room
- the filing cabinet sits close enough to the radiator side to be part of the same pressure cluster
- shelves, files, or storage sit near the edges rather than opening the room up
- there is a slight bad patch in the floor near the radiator side that careful movement may need to negotiate
- a kitchenette or coffee corner sits off to one side rather than opening the room into a separate second space
- walking from door to desk or from desk to guest position should feel like crossing limited, inconvenient ground rather than a generous open floor

Do not treat this as blueprint precision. Treat it as stable scene geometry.

---

# 6. Recurring Objects

Objects that may recur without needing to be identical every time:

- damp papers or files
- pencil, notebook, or writing clutter
- weak office lighting, often with one lamp doing more work than it should
- blanket or other evidence of Dragon half-living in the workspace
- small treasure objects kept near the desk or shelf area
- cups, coffee equipment, or procedural clutter when Dragon is trying to regain control
- filing-cabinet-adjacent drift, such as pillows, papers, or things that no longer fully belong anywhere else

These objects should help stage scenes, not become mandatory checklist props.

---

# 7. Relationship To Dragon

The office reflects Dragon without becoming a blunt symbol.

Shared traits:

- visibly worn
- still functional
- embarrassing in detail
- stubbornly serviceable
- patched together through continued use rather than elegant restoration

The office should expose:

- Dragon's failed dignity
- his procedural habits
- his professional vanity
- his comfort-seeking
- his tolerance for practical decline so long as the place still mostly works

It is one of the main spaces where Dragon turns embarrassment into procedure.

---

# 8. Scene Use

The office is especially good for scenes involving:

- threshold shifts from weather into story
- procedural recovery after embarrassment or destabilization
- client interviews
- coffee-as-procedure
- professionalism being sabotaged by material reality
- quiet recognition scenes

When using the office, prefer:

- physical consequence
- object behaviour
- environmental interruption
- small indignities
- practical negotiation with space

Do not use the office as:

- a generic detective backdrop
- a purely comic mess
- a sentimental nest
- a stage set that stops affecting the scene once described

---

# 9. What Can Move vs What Stays Stable

What can move:

- the exact clutter
- which papers are dampest
- what loose object becomes a nuisance
- where a blanket, bowl, cup, or notebook happens to be
- how much temporary disorder one bad week has added

What should stay stable:

- the office is cramped
- the desk is inconvenient
- weather enters through the window side
- the radiator is weak
- the radiator/window/filing-cabinet side is the room's main pressure side
- the room resists clean professionalism
- visitors must be spatially negotiated, not simply welcomed into open space

---

# 10. Writing Guidance

Do not require every office scene to repeat the same inventory of props.

Instead, preserve the stable logic:

- the office is compromised by weather
- it resists clean professionalism
- Dragon keeps working in it anyway
- the room participates in scenes through drafts, dampness, clutter, furniture, paper, and heat failure

Use different local details each time, but keep the same emotional and practical truth.
